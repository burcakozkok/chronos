# Style Guide — Carl Sagan Narrative Voice

## Core Principles

1. **Wonder and Awe:** Open every chapter or section with a sense of cosmic perspective. Remind the reader that human civilization is a brief, precious flicker in the vast expanse of time and space.

2. **Plain Language:** Use clear, accessible prose. Avoid jargon. When technical terms are necessary, explain them immediately in plain language.

3. **Active Voice:** Prefer active over passive constructions. Sentences should move forward with energy and purpose.

4. **Poetic Rhythm:** Vary sentence length deliberately. Short sentences for emphasis. Longer, flowing sentences for context and reflection. Read aloud to check rhythm.

5. **Humanistic Perspective:** Always return to the human scale. Statistics and abstractions must be grounded in the lived experience of real people.

6. **Scientific Rigor:** Every claim should be supportable. Speculation is permitted — and encouraged — but must be clearly flagged as such.

7. **Cosmic Humility:** Acknowledge uncertainty. The future is not written. Our choices matter precisely because outcomes are not predetermined.

## Signature Sagan Devices

- **"Consider..." or "Imagine..."** — Open sections by inviting the reader into a thought experiment.
- **The Long View** — Place events in deep historical or cosmic context.
- **The Pale Blue Dot** — Periodically zoom out to remind the reader of civilizational fragility and preciousness.
- **The Rhetorical Question** — Use questions to guide the reader toward insight rather than stating conclusions bluntly.
- **Repetition for Emphasis** — Key phrases repeated with variation build emotional resonance.

## Tone
- Curious, not didactic
- Hopeful, not naive
- Urgent, not alarmist
- Humble, not uncertain

## What to Avoid
- Bullet points in the main essay text (use prose)
- Passive constructions where active is possible
- Unexplained acronyms or jargon
- Dry, academic register
- Excessive hedging or qualification
