# Chapter 1 Context — The Long Arc of Civilization

## Scope
This chapter establishes the foundational arc of human civilizational history, tracing the journey from hunter-gatherer societies through slavery, feudalism, and capitalism. It introduces the core thesis that technological innovation is the primary trigger for transitions between civilizational stages.

## Key Themes
- Hunter-gatherer societies: communal, nomadic, egalitarian
- Agricultural revolution: settled communities, surplus, hierarchy
- Slavery-based societies: class division, exploitation
- Feudalism: serfs and lords, land-bound labor
- Industrial capitalism: machines, wage labor, urban growth
- Technology as the engine of civilizational change
- Co-evolution of technology and society

## Revolutions to Weave In
- Cognitive Revolution (~70,000 BCE)
- Agricultural Revolution (~10,000 BCE)
- Urban Revolution (~3,500 BCE)
- Bronze Age Collapse (~1,200 BCE) and its role as precursor to the Axial Age
- Axial Age / Philosophical Revolution (~800–200 BCE)
- Scientific Revolution (~1543–1687)
- Industrial Revolution (~1760–1840)
